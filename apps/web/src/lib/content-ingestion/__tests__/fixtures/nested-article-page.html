Tracing a campus collection

Archivists began the survey in a basement room where decades of field notebooks had been arranged by expedition rather than by date. The first task was to connect each handwritten catalog number with the specimen that eventually reached the university collection.

That work uncovered a sequence of annotations showing how researchers revised their understanding of the collection over time. Marginal notes recorded changes in naming, habitat descriptions, and the locations where later teams found related material.

A record that kept changing

The new digital record preserves those corrections instead of flattening them into a single authoritative description. Readers can follow the evidence from the original notebook through later labels and the current catalog entry.

Students are now using the archive to compare historical collection practices with current field methods. Their findings will guide the next phase of digitization and determine which fragile notebooks receive conservation treatment first.
