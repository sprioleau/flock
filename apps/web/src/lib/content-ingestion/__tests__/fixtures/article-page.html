City to build solar canopy over downtown parking

By Dana Reeve

The city council voted 8-1 on Tuesday to approve construction of a 4.2-megawatt solar canopy spanning the Fifth Street parking garage, a project officials described as the largest municipal solar installation in the state.

The canopy, designed by local firm Meridian Energy Works, will cover roughly six acres of open-air parking and is expected to generate enough electricity to power about 900 homes. Construction is scheduled to begin in October and finish within fourteen months.

Mayor Alicia Fontaine called the vote a turning point for the city's climate plan. "We are turning our hottest, emptiest asphalt into shade and power at the same time," she said at a press conference after the vote.

How the project will be paid for

Financing combines a $12 million federal clean-energy grant with a power-purchase agreement that locks the city into a fixed electricity rate for twenty years. City analysts project net savings of $18 million over the life of the agreement.

The lone dissenting vote came from Councilman Ray Ortiz, who argued the garage roof should have been reserved for a long-discussed transit hub. "Once the panels go up, that option is gone for a generation," Ortiz said during the hearing.

What happens next

Permitting is expected to take the rest of the summer. During construction, about 400 of the garage's 2,300 spaces will close on a rolling basis, and the city will offer displaced monthly permit holders discounted spots at the Riverside lot.

Meridian Energy Works must also complete a structural study confirming the 1970s-era garage can carry the added load, a requirement added after residents raised concerns at June's public comment session.
